Past simple · the whole picture

We use the past simple for finished past actions and states.

RegularIrregular
+I playedI wore
−I didn't play / wear (infinitive!)
?Did you play / wear…?
The classic mistake: after did / didn't the verb goes back to its base form. ❌ I didn't went → ✅ I didn't go. ❌ Did you saw? → ✅ Did you see?

Spelling of -ed

  • most verbs: watch → watched
  • ends in e: use → used
  • short vowel + consonant: plan → planned, stop → stopped
  • consonant + y: study → studied (but enjoyed — vowel + y)

Time expressions

  • yesterday · last night/week · two days ago · in 1969 · when I was young

Adverbs of manner

Adjectives describe nouns. Adverbs describe verbs — how we do something.

RuleAdjectiveAdverb
+ lypolitepolitely
y → ilyheavyheavily
irregulargoodwell
no changefast · hard · earlyfast · hard · early
Watch out: hardly is not the adverb of hard! He works hard = a lot of effort. He hardly works = almost never 😅
After be, look, feel, seem we use an adjective, not an adverb: She looks happy. / She smiled happily.

Prepare to speak · talking about yourself

  • Habits — present simple + frequency adverbs: I usually… · I always… · I often…
  • Reasons — …because…
  • Likes & dislikes — I like… · I don't like… · I really like…
  • Opinion — I think… · I don't think…
🎯 A one-word answer scores nothing. The formula that always works: answer + reason + example.

Culture · The Beatles & the 60s

  • 🎸 Four musicians from Liverpool: John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison, Ringo Starr.
  • 📺 In the early 1960s British TV was still black and white — colour came at the end of the decade.
  • 💇 Their early look: matching suits and "mop-top" haircuts. By 1967–69: long hair, beards, bright, loose clothes.
  • 💿 Abbey Road (1969) — the famous zebra-crossing cover, one of their last albums.
  • 👗 60s fashion: mini skirts, colourful tights, bold patterns — a real break from parents' style.

FSpeaking · the interview game 🎤 +25 XP

In pairs. One is a reporter from 1969, the other is a teenager of today.

  • The reporter asks about clothes, music and shopping today — all in the present simple.
  • The teenager answers, then asks the reporter what people wore, listened to and bought in the 60s — all in the past simple.
  • Swap roles. Aim for six questions each.
