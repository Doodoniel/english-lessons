Word Bank · Describing people

Hover over a word to see what it means. These are the answers to Ex. 2, p.10.

Hair bald blonde curly dark fair straight
Watch out! fair hair is light hair in general; blonde is very light hair. And we don't say he is beautiful — use handsome or good-looking.

Grammar spotlight

1 · Appearance: have got

  • She's got straight hair. = She has got straight hair.
  • He's got dark curly hair. → ❌ He has dark hairs — hair is uncountable!
  • Question: Has she got fair hair? — Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

2 · Personality: be

  • He is confident. She is never miserable.

3 · Order of adjectives

Opinion → Size → Age → Colour + NOUN
a nice tall teenage boy · long dark curly hair

4 · Adverbs of frequency

  • She always says hello. → before the main verb
  • He's sometimes a bit careless. → after the verb be

Adverbs · how they work

  • + ly: careful → carefully, bad → badly
  • y → ily: heavy → heavily, healthy → healthily, easy → easily
  • No change: fast → fast, hard → hard, early → early, late → late, yearly → yearly
  • Irregular: good → well
Careful: hardly does not mean "with a lot of effort". He works hard = he makes a big effort. He hardly works = he almost never works 😅
look + adjective: in "Georgina looks really well", well is an adjective meaning "healthy", not an adverb. That's the trap in Ex. 5 on p.11.

Culture corner · PROM

  • 🇺🇸 A prom (from promenade) is a formal dance for students at the end of high school in the USA.
  • 🇬🇧 In Britain people traditionally say school leavers' ball or end-of-year party, but the American word prom is now common too — that's exactly what Rob and Lara argue about.
  • 👔 Dress code: a tuxedo (or tux) with a bow tie for boys; a prom dress and a corsage (a small flower worn on the wrist) for girls.
  • 👑 Students vote for a prom king and prom queen, and some arrive in a limo.
  • 💸 An American family often spends hundreds of dollars on prom — that's why Rob asks if a dress is worth so much money.
Useful: to ask someone to the prom · a promposal (a creative way of inviting someone) · the afterparty

Extra challenge · Writing

  • Bronze: write 4 sentences about someone in your family (looks + personality + an example of their behaviour).
  • Silver: add two adverbs from p.11 and one always or never.
  • Gold: describe two people and compare them: My brother is more confident than my sister, but she's friendlier.
  • Platinum 😈: describe someone without using a single appearance adjective — only through what they do.
