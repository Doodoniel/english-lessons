⚡ The three questions

  • 1 Countable? If uncountable → no a/an, singular verb: some advice is…
  • 2 Which one? If the listener knows → the. If it is new → a/an.
  • 3 In general? Plural or uncountable in general → no article: I prefer films to books.
Never: an advice · informations · the most people · go to the bed

Unit 1 · Nouns

📘 Theory · Unit 1

1 · Types of noun & capital letters
Nouns name people, things and places (a tourist, a postcard, a museum), and also activities, ideas and feelings (shopping, information, love).

Capital letters for names of people, places, dates, events, religions, languages, books, plays and films:

Professor Grey · the British Museum · Glasgow · Africa · Monday · April · May Day · Russian · Spanish · Islam · ‘The Lord of the Rings’ · ‘War and Peace’
2 · Singular and plural forms
+ -smost nouns: books, postcards, days, houses
+ -esafter -s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -o, -x, -z: buses, dishes, watches, potatoes, boxes  BUT videos, pianos
consonant + -y → -iescountry → countries, family → families
-f / -fe → -veslife → lives, thief → thieves, loaf → loaves
irregularman → men · woman → women · child → children · person → people · foot → feet · tooth → teeth
no changeaircraft · series · fish · sheep
3 · Noun + verb: singular or plural?
-s but singularathletics, economics, gymnastics, politics, maths, physics, news
Athletics is an Olympic sport. · Today’s news isn’t very exciting.
two parts → pluraljeans, pants, pyjamas, shorts, tights, trousers, glasses, scissors
These jeans are too tight. Counting: a pair of trousers, three pairs of shorts
group nounscompany, family, government, team → singular or plural in British English:
The Spanish team is / are playing brilliantly.
Only singular when the group is one thing: A football team have eleven players. ✓ A football team has eleven players.
always pluralpolice: The police has arrested… ✓ The police have arrested the thieves.
4 · Countable and uncountable nouns
Uncountable: food and drink (bread, butter, rice), materials (water, wood, plastic, paper), school subjects and languages, ideas and feelings (education, work, advice, knowledge), activities (shopping, cycling, swimming), groups of similar things (furniture, luggage, money).
Countableone shop → two shops · a/an, the, this/that · Is there a museum here?
Uncountableno plural: two petrols → some petrol · singular verb: Swimming is a popular sport. · no a/an: Can you give me an advice? ✓ some advice
5 · Nouns that can be both
countable meaninguncountable meaning
six chickens in the garden (animals)chicken and rice (food)
a coffee, two teas (cups)lemon tea (the drink)
a paper (a newspaper)writing paper (material)
easy exercises (tasks)do much exercise (physical activity)
interesting experiences (events)experience is important (learning by doing)
two works by Goya (paintings)my work (job)
6 · How to count uncountable nouns
unitsa bar of soap · a bit of information/fun · an item of news · a piece of furniture/luggage/paper · a sheet of paper · a slice of bread/cake/meat
containersa bottle of water · a can/tin of soup · a carton of milk · a cup of coffee · a glass of juice · a jar of jam · a packet of sugar · a tub of butter · a tube of toothpaste
measurementshalf a kilo of meat · a litre of petrol · two metres of silk

Unit 2 · Articles a/an, the, no article

📘 Theory · Unit 2

1 · a or an? · the /ðə/ or /ðiː/?
a before consonant sounds: a café, a tourist, a museum — and before u/eu when they sound like /juː/: a uniform, a European city.
an before vowel sounds: an apple, an interesting book, an unusual job — and before silent h: an hour /ˈaʊə/.
Pronunciation of the: /ðə/ before consonant sounds (the book, the tourist), /ðiː/ before vowel sounds (the art gallery, the English teacher).
It is the sound, not the letter: an MP /em/, a university /juː/.
2 · General or particular meaning?
generala/an + singular: Is there a café here? (any café)
no article + plural: Sandwiches aren’t expensive. (all sandwiches)
no article + uncountable: I don’t like music. (all music)
particularthe + singular: The café’s on the ground floor.
the + plural: Did you get the sandwiches?
the + uncountable: I don’t like the music on that CD.
No the for general meaning: I prefer the films to the books. → I prefer films to books.
3 · New information or known information?
First mention → a/an. Second mention → the.
I’ve just bought a new suit. It’s the grey suit we saw in the shop window.
‘Carla asked her teacher for advice.’ ‘Was the advice useful?’
We also use the when the listener already knows which one we mean: It was a great holiday. Have you seen the photos yet?

Unit 3 · Special uses of a/an and the

📘 Theory · Unit 3

1 · Jobs and descriptions
a/an with jobs: Michelle’s a student. I want to be an engineer.
a/an to describe a person or thing, usually with an adjective: She’s copying a beautiful painting. He’s a nice person. That was an interesting exhibition.
2 · Institutions: purpose or building?
school · college · university · prison · hospital
purpose → no articlebuilding → the / a
Michelle is a student at art school.The art school is in North Street.
My brother’s sick in hospital.Excuse me, where’s the hospital?
The murderer was sent to prison.The prison has stone walls.
3 · The media
the + most newspapers: the Daily News, the Sun, the New York Times.
no article + most magazines: Vogue, Newsweek, Time, Hello.
‘Did you see her photo in Vogue?’ ‘No, it was in the Sun.’
We say the cinema, the theatre, the radio — but television / TV with no article when we mean the programmes: In the mornings I listen to the radio, but in the evenings I prefer watching TV. The machine takes an article: There’s something wrong with the television in my bedroom.
4 · the + adjective = a group of people
the young · the old · the elderly · the rich · the poor · the sick · the homeless · the disabled · the unemployed · the blind · the deaf · the injured · the dead — always with a plural verb: The rich are getting richer and the poor are getting poorer.
Nationalities ending in -ch / -sh / -ese work the same way: The French have elected a new president. The Irish are voting tomorrow.
Other nationalities do not: The Brazilian are used to hot weather. → Brazilians are used to hot weather.

📘 Theory · Unit 4

1 · No article
people’s names and titlesDavid · Mr Brown · Professor Smith · Uncle John
shops and companiesHarrods · Tesco · Microsoft · United Airlines
cities, states, regionsLondon · Sydney · Madrid · Florida · Tuscany
most countries and continentsRussia · Japan · Turkey · Australia · Asia · Europe · Africa
mountains, hills, lakesMount Vesuvius · Box Hill · Lake Placid
roads, streets, squares, parksPark Lane · Oxford Street · Trafalgar Square · Central Park (BUT the M25, the high street)
airports and stationsMiami Airport · Birmingham Bus Station · Victoria Station
schools, universitiesBradford Grammar School · Yale University
times, days, monthsat three o’clock · at midday · on Monday · in December
languages, subjects, sportsEnglish · history · basketball · tennis · football
transport and communicationby train · by bus · by email · by phone (BUT on foot, in person)
mealsWhat time is dinner? · Let’s meet after lunch. (BUT a snack)
noun + numberGo to check-in 3A · I’m in Room 334 · Look at page 98
Also: at home · at work · in bed · at school.
2 · the
oceans, seas, rivers, mountain rangesthe Atlantic Ocean · the Black Sea · the Seine · the Nile · the Himalayas · the Alps
plural / Republic / Kingdom countriesthe United States · the Netherlands · the Republic of Ireland · the United Kingdom
organisationsthe International Monetary Fund · the World Bank
museums, cinemas, theatres, hotelsthe Getty Museum · the Odeon Cinema · the National Theatre · the Ritz Hotel
places in a town, types of shopthe bank · the hospital · the post office · the station · the supermarket · the chemist’s
locationson the left/right · in the middle · on the coast/border · in the east/north
parts of a buildingthe ground floor · the fifth floor · the exit · the entrance
times of the dayin the morning · in the afternoon · in the evening (BUT at night)
historical periodsthe nineteenth century · the 1950s · the Middle Ages
musical instruments (BrE)Does she play the guitar? Can you play the violin?
noun + ofthe House of Commons · the University of London · the Isle of Man
3 · a/an = one · each
one with fractions and large numbers: half a kilometre · one and a half · a thousand
each for frequency and measurements: once a day · twice a month · six euros a kilo · 100 kilometres an hour (BUT every hour)
