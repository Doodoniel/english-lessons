💡 Quick reminders

  • After did / didn't → infinitive: didn't go.
  • One comparative only: ❌ more taller → taller.
  • who = people · which = things.
  • Adverb describes a verb: speaks quietly. Adjective after be / look / seem: looks quiet.
  • Plural-only clothes: jeans, trousers, trainers — they are.

Part C · Pronunciation
7

The -ed ending: three sounds

+60 XPsound sortingPronunciation

Say each verb out loud, then put it in the right column. -ed is written the same way but pronounced three different ways.

/ɪd/ — only after t or d (wanted, decided) · adds a syllable
/t/ — after quiet sounds: p, k, f, s, sh, ch (stopped, watched)
/d/ — after all the rest, including vowels (played, listened)

/ɪd/wanted

/t/stopped

/d/played

Part D · Reading

Reading: "The jacket that travelled"

+70 XPTrue / False / Doesn't sayReading

Read the article once, then decide. Choose Doesn't say when the text simply does not give you the information — that is not the same as False.

The jacket that travelled 🧥

Maya Osei is sixteen and lives in Bristol. Two years ago, her grandmother gave her an old denim jacket. It belonged to Maya's grandfather, who bought it in 1979.

At first Maya didn't wear it. "It was too big and the colour was horrible," she says. "I put it at the back of my wardrobe and forgot about it." Then, last winter, she saw a photo of a singer in a similar jacket. Suddenly the jacket looked interesting.

Maya decided to change it. She cut the sleeves, added colourful buttons and painted a small flower on the back. It took her three weekends. When she wore it to school, six people asked her where she bought it.

Now Maya repairs and redesigns old clothes for her friends. She doesn't charge much — usually about five pounds. "I don't do it for the money," she explains. "I do it because I hate throwing clothes away. My grandfather wore this jacket for twenty years. Why should I buy a new one?"

Maya's mother thinks she should study fashion at university. Maya isn't sure yet.
