    3A · The forms
    + regular
    verb + -ed
    They danced until midnight.
    He walked up four floors.
    + irregular
    a new word
    bring → brought
    come → came · eat → ate
    − and ?
    did / didn’t + base verb
    She didn’t shout.
    Did Max spill the juice?
    The golden rule: after did and didn’t the verb goes back to its base form — didn’t came come, Did you went go?. Only be is different: was / were, wasn’t / weren’t, Was it…?
    Spelling of -ed: dance → danced (just -d) · stop → stopped (double letter) · carry → carried (-y → -ied) · play → played (vowel + y stays).
    Нужна опора на русском?

    Past Simple — законченное действие в прошлом: вчера, на прошлой неделе, в семь часов. Правильные глаголы: + -ed. Неправильные надо запомнить. Отрицание и вопрос: didn’t / did + глагол без окончания. Глагол be живёт отдельно: was / were.

    ⏱ 9 min
    4A · The text

    The Morning After

    Emma’s blog · ≈ 230 words

    The party started at seven o’clock. Nine people came, and everybody brought something: Kate brought the music, Max carried the drinks up four floors, and Nina arrived with an enormous chocolate cake.

    At half past seven the doorbell rang again. It was the pizza delivery man, and he wasn’t happy: the lift was still broken, so he walked up to the fourth floor with six pizzas. Emma gave him a slice of cake and he laughed.

    The evening was noisy. We played music, danced in the middle of the living room and talked until midnight. Only one thing went wrong: Max spilled orange juice on my new yellow wall, and everybody stopped and looked at him. I didn’t shout. I said, “Now the wall has a story,” and the party continued.

    The next morning the flat looked terrible. There were glasses on the bookshelf, a shoe under the sofa and half a pizza in the wardrobe. I didn’t tidy up alone: at ten o’clock the doorbell rang again, and Kate and Max came back with coffee and rubber gloves. We cleaned everything in two hours. Then we sat on the floor and ate the last pizza for breakfast. It wasn’t a normal Sunday morning, but it was the best one this year.

    Teacher notes · lesson design
    Why the Past Simple now
    Lesson 9 planned the party with be going to; this lesson tells the same story after the event. The context is already known, so the whole cognitive load can go on the new form.
    Ten-minute spiral review
    Section 1 touches every earlier lesson: there is/are, prepositions, the three cameras, linking words, adverbs. The spinner makes the review oral, which is where the gaps actually show.
    Vocabulary before grammar
    The party verbs are taught in the present first (word search, step-by-step, definitions), so that in section 3 the learner only changes the form, not the meaning.
    Form and sound together
    The -ed sorting game continues the pronunciation lab of Lesson 9: A2 learners who never hear the three endings tend to add a syllable to every regular verb.
    Seven mechanics
    Oral spinner, lightning quiz, error hunt, word search, step-by-step choice, sorting into sound groups, transformations — plus reading, three speaking formats and the writing task.
    Speaking builds up
    Retell a known story → ask questions with did → invent your own three sentences. Support drops at each step, which is the standard scaffolding sequence.
    Homework as requested
    Three vocabulary tasks, three grammar tasks and one integrated writing task; everything auto-marked except the writing, which arrives on the answer sheet with the marked items.
    Assessment for learning
    Retry-friendly hints, visible success criteria, and a sheet that shows the teacher what the page accepted — so lesson time goes to the open task.
    If time is short
    Do 1A orally, keep 3A–3C and 3E, read the story, and choose one speaking task. Move 2A, 3D and 3F to the homework.
