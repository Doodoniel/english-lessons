Grammar spotlight

1 · wear · wearing · in

  • She is wearing a black shirt. → right now
  • She wears black a lot. → generally, a habit
  • The girl in the red jacket is my sister. → identifying someone
  • ❌ She is wearing a black → an adjective needs its noun: a black shirt

2 · Order of adjectives

Opinion → Size → Age → Colour → Material + NOUN
a lovely skinny brand-new black leather jacket

3 · Plural-only clothes

  • trousers, jeans, shorts, trainers, sunglasses are always plural: These jeans are too skinny. ❌ This jeans is
  • To count them: a pair of jeans, two pairs of trainers.

4 · look + adjective

  • He looks smart. · The suit looks brand new. · You look like a rock star. (+ noun)

Fashion timeline · from the article

  • Before mid-1950s — no youth culture; teens dressed and listened like their parents.
  • 1954 — Rock Around the Clock, Bill Haley and the Comets. Smart suits, skinny ties (Buddy Holly, Elvis). Loose-fitting skirts for dancing.
  • 1960s — The Rolling Stones; very short mini skirts shock the older generation.
  • Mid 60s–mid 70s — the hippie era: long hair, flowery clothing, sandals; The Beatles, Joni Mitchell.
  • Late 70s — punk: second-hand clothes, often torn; colourful, messy hair. The Clash, Debbie Harry, Patti Smith.
  • 1980s — long curly hair, bright colours; Michael Jackson, Madonna, U2.
  • 1990s — boy bands and girl bands; hip hop tracksuits, gold rings and necklaces.
  • Today — YouTubers, superstar DJs; fashions "here today and gone tomorrow".

Useful language · for speaking

Describing what you see

  • He's wearing… / She's got… on. · It looks like…
  • In this photo she seems quite… · The trousers look a bit…

Giving an opinion

  • Personally, I'd never wear… · I quite like… but I prefer…
  • It's just not my style. · That really suits him.

Agreeing & disagreeing politely

  • You've got a point, but… · I see what you mean, though…
  • Exactly! That's what I think too.
