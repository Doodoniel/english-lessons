🎯 Today's aims

By the end of this lesson I can…
  • sort words for appearance, personality and clothes;
  • compare people and styles (comparatives & superlatives);
  • join ideas with who / which / that;
  • read a blog post and understand gist and detail;
  • hear and mark word stress;
  • proofread and fix common mistakes;
  • write and talk about a person I admire.

📘 Grammar reference

Comparatives & superlatives

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
shortshorterthe shortest
friendlyfriendlierthe friendliest
confidentmore confidentthe most confident
goodbetterthe best
…er than · more … than · one adjective only: ❌ more taller → taller.

Relative pronouns

  • who → people: the girl who is wearing a cap
  • which → things: a jacket which is second-hand
  • that → people or things (informal)

🗣️ Useful language

Comparing

  • …is more … than… · …isn't as … as… · Both of them are… · The main difference is…

Opinion & reason

  • I admire her because… · What I like most is… · The reason is that…

Reacting

  • Really? Why do you say that? · That's a good point. · I feel the same.

4

Reading: "The coolest person I know"

+60 XPReading

First read the blog post once for the general idea (gist). Then answer the questions.

My grandma is the coolest person I know 💛

Everyone talks about celebrities and YouTubers, but the most stylish person I know is my grandma, Rosa. She's in her late seventies, she's got short silver hair, and she always wears bright colours. People think older people dress in boring clothes, but Rosa is different. She loves second-hand shops, and she often finds jackets which are more interesting than anything in the shopping centre.

Rosa is also the most confident person in our family. She's friendly and funny, and she talks to everyone. My friends say she is cooler than most teenagers! When I feel shy, she always tells me the same thing: "Wear what makes you happy, not what makes other people happy."

Last month she wore a bright yellow coat and green boots to my school concert. Some people looked surprised, but I felt proud. My grandma taught me that style isn't about being young or rich — it's about being yourself.

Write: a person you admire

+30 XPWriting

Write about a real person you admire — like Rosa in the reading. Use the plan.

Sentence 1–2 — Who is the person and how do you know them?
Sentence 3–4 — Appearance & clothes (use 2 adjectives from Stage 1).
Sentence 5–6 — Personality + one comparative (…is more … than…).
Sentence 7 — Why you admire them (…because…).
0 wordstarget: 60–90 words
Self-check before you finish: comparative ✓ · relative pronoun (who/which) ✓ · a reason with because ✓ · full stops and capital letters ✓
8

Speak: two mini-tasks

+40 XPSpeaking

Work in pairs. Use the Useful language box on the left. Tick a task when you finish it.

A🕵️ Guess who. Describe a classmate without saying their name, using a relative clause: "I'm thinking of the person who is wearing…". Your partner guesses. Swap and repeat.

B⏱️ One-minute talk. Talk for one minute about your style icon. Compare them with someone else: "She's more creative than…". Your partner asks one follow-up question at the end.
