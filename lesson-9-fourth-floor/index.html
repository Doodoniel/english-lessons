Because · so · but · although

Until now you built single sentences. Today you join them. These four words are what turns a list of facts into a story — and they are exactly what the writer of today’s text uses.

⏱ 12 min
3A · Four jobs, four words
reason · why?
because
She isn’t going to cook because the kitchen is full of boxes.
result · what happens then?
so
The lift is broken, so everybody uses the stairs.
contrast · a surprise
but
The rooms are small, but they are full of light.
strong contrast · start or middle
although
Although the flat is small, Emma loves it.
Emma loves the flat although it is small.
Watch the order: because and although are followed by the reason or the surprising fact. so is always followed by the result — you cannot start an English sentence with So in writing at this level.
Нужна опора на русском?

because — потому что (причина). so — поэтому (результат). but — но (противопоставление). although — хотя (сильное противопоставление; ставится в начале предложения или перед второй частью). Запятая ставится перед but и so, а перед because — обычно нет.

4B · The text · blog post

The Flat on the Fourth Floor

student blog · ≈ 390 words · four paragraphs

1. Emma’s new flat is on the fourth floor of an old building near the river. The rooms are small, but they are full of light. There are three of them: a bedroom, a living room and a narrow kitchen with a window that looks over the roofs of the city. In the bedroom there is a bed under the window and, next to the door, a tall wardrobe that the old owner left behind. One of its doors doesn’t close properly, but Emma isn’t going to throw it away — it is the only piece of furniture she didn’t pay for. Between the two living-room windows there is a bookshelf with all her photos on it. That corner is her favourite place in the flat.

2. Her weekdays are always the same. She wakes up at half past six, has breakfast standing in the kitchen and takes the number 12 bus to the office. She gets home late, so she usually cooks something quick, tidies up and reads until she falls asleep. Saturday is different: she does the shopping early and then meets her friend Kate in the park, because it is the only morning when nobody needs anything from her.

3. This week, however, there is no routine at all. Right now, Emma isn’t sitting on the sofa with a book. She is standing in the middle of the living room with a paintbrush in her hand, and the grey walls are slowly turning yellow. Her brother Max is putting up a shelf — with the wrong screws, but nobody is telling him. Kate is cleaning windows that nobody cleaned for years. There are boxes in every corner and nobody is relaxing, but everybody is laughing.

4. On Sunday, Emma is going to have a housewarming party. Nine friends are going to come at seven o’clock, and there is one small problem: the lift is broken and the flat is on the fourth floor. She isn’t going to cook, because the kitchen is still full of boxes, so she is going to order pizza — the delivery man is not going to enjoy the stairs. Kate is going to bring music and Max is going to carry the drinks up. The forecast says rain, but nobody minds: the party is indoors, and there is nowhere else Emma wants to be on Sunday evening.

Teacher notes · lesson design
Continuity
Lesson 8 ran out of time before the reading and the speaking, so both stages are carried over unchanged. Everything before them was rebuilt so that nothing is repeated twice.
Pre-teaching before reading
Section 2 front-loads exactly the words that make the text hard (narrow, roofs, lift, broken, screws, delivery, properly, slowly). This is standard practice for lowering the reading load without simplifying the text.
One new language step
Linking words are the logical next step after single-sentence grammar: they move the learner from sentence level to discourse level, which the CEFR A2 descriptors ask for in both writing and speaking.
Retrieval before instruction
The sprint is timed retrieval of everything from Lessons 4–8; the homework opens with retrieval too. Testing beats re-reading for long-term memory.
Reading skills ladder
Gist → detail (T/F/Doesn’t say) → inference → vocabulary in context → evidence in full sentences.
Seven homework mechanics
Quiz, word formation, gap-fill with linkers, real-world text comprehension, error correction, guided writing, and spoken production with reflection.
Assessment for learning
Objective tasks are auto-marked with retry-friendly hints; the answer sheet shows the teacher both the answer and whether the app accepted it, so feedback time goes to the open tasks.
Learner autonomy
The reflection box (H7) makes the student name their own difficulty — the first step of self-regulated learning.
If time is short
Keep 1A, 3A–3B, and the full reading. Move section 2C, 3D and the role-play to the next lesson; the homework already recycles them.
